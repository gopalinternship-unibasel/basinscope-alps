What other thresholds would have said

A test, not a calibration. Every day is judged on the forecast for that day, kept in the forecast archive of Open-Meteo, and worked out as in the outlook above: the highest zero-degree level and the mean precipitation of the 15 site locations. The archive serves the best model it has for each date, so the model changes over the years; the live outlook uses MeteoSwiss ICON-CH2. A thunderstorm cell that misses the 15 points is not seen, and a multi-day event is judged on its strongest day. Four events cannot set a threshold: they show that the rule reacts on days that mattered, and the yearly counts show how often it reacts on days that did not.

Why this matters here

Blatten, 28 May 2025

The Birch glacier above Blatten in the Lötschental collapsed on 28 May 2025. Ice and rock buried most of the evacuated village and dammed the Lonza. The federal gauge at Blatten has sent no reading since that day.

Three of the 15 ranked sites lie in that valley. Blatten is excluded as a settlement area, and the terrain analysis notes that the ground there has changed fundamentally. Fafleralp and the Jegigletscher forefield lie upstream of it.

  • Shows where federal index maps place a process within 250 m of a site
  • Shows when thaw or heavy precipitation is forecast there
  • States every threshold and lets you move it
  • Predict an event or its size
  • Cover avalanches or glacier collapse

Avalanches are left out because the federal index map only models avalanches that start in forest, which says nothing above the tree line. The hazard index maps date from 2005 and 2006.

Stress test: the hydropower on these rivers

A first-order stress test, not a plant model. The plants are matched to the nine catchments by name and location on the map; the match is this dashboard's and has not been checked with the operators. A run-of-river plant follows the monthly runoff up to its turbine capacity. A storage plant is shown by the energy of the water that reaches it, because its reservoir decides when that water is turbined. Every plant is scaled so that the reference runoff of 1981–2010 gives its expected production in the SFOE statistics. Runoff: Hydro-CH2018 at the gauge of each catchment, ensemble median; the range takes the lowest and the highest model month by month. Plants further down a river also take water from below the gauge, and pumping is left out.

Does the ranking hold up?

Evidence layers

    A site cannot reach high confidence until the open layers are checked by a specialist.

    Set against the federal report of 2024

    • Finds sites from the terrain itself, so it also ranks places no operator has proposed
    • Weighs storage, the late-summer gap, dam length, footprint and protection with weights anyone can move
    • Asks the federal inventories for the protection status of each site, and re-asks on demand
    • Responds to the runoff period, the emissions path and the glacier size, and shows the region's state today
    • Cover Switzerland: the report lists projects in four cantons, this is one region
    • Carry a design for each project: head, power chain and a dam sized by the operator
    • Rank the Unterer Grindelwaldgletscher or the Oberaletsch, which the report lists
    • Replace the weighing of interests that the report leaves to the authorities

    reference architecture for a federal roll-out
    Nationwide todayFederal open data
    • swissALTI3D terrain, SGI glacier inventory
    • Hydro-CH2018 runoff scenarios
    • Protection inventories, hazard index maps
    • Hydropower plants, dams, reservoir storage
    • Live gauges, weather stations, ICON forecast
    • Sentinel-2 satellite mosaics, every few days
    Repeated per regionTerrain run
    • Flow routing and basin search in GRASS, on a study-area polygon
    • Checks against the federal layers for each site found
    • The study area is one polygon file: a new region is a new polygon
    One per regionData file and open API
    • A single JSON dataset per region
    • Static JSON API with an OpenAPI description
    • No server to run, any static host
    SharedDecision interface
    • This dashboard, reading the region's dataset
    • Live federal feeds asked from the browser
    • GIS, utilities and offices reading the API
    • Every source already covers the whole country, so no new data has to be bought or collected
    • The dashboard and the API are static files: a federal or cantonal host can serve them as they are
    • Data sovereignty: the live measurements, maps and satellite scenes come straight from Swiss federal services, and no account, key or tracker stands between a visitor and the data
    • Jungfrau–Aletsch is the first region, with 303 of the about 961 km² of Swiss glacier ice (SGI 2016)
    • The terrain run has been done for this one region only and is not yet a repeatable job
    • Site names and the match to Hydro-CH2018 catchments are not automated yet
    • The forecast comes through Open-Meteo, free for non-commercial use: a federal service would read the MeteoSwiss open data itself
    • This demonstration is hosted on GitHub Pages; the same files can move to a Swiss host unchanged

    Method limits. Surface runoff only (D8 routing on the 10 m swissALTI3D mean); subglacial flow and karst are missing. Meltwater is glacier area × the melt rate, with no rain or snowmelt from outside the glaciers. Glacier outlines (2013–2018) are older than the terrain model (2024/25), so forefields that have become ice-free since were not tested. Volumes tend to be slightly too small and dam length is indicative.
